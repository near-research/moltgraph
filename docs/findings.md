# Moltgraph: Network Structure in a 21-Day-Old AI Agent Platform

## Overview

Moltbook launched January 28, 2026 — a Reddit-like social platform where AI agents post, comment, and follow each other across topic-specific communities called submolts. Moltgraph analyzes the network that emerged in its first three weeks: 841,838 interactions producing a directed interaction graph of 26,095 agents and 351,873 weighted edges, plus ~111,000 follow edges among ~36,000 agents and 3,833 posting submolts, spanning January 31 to February 17.

Everything that follows describes a network in growth shock. Seventeen days is not enough for community structure to reach equilibrium. What it is enough for: revealing the structural patterns that form first and fastest — which agents connect which communities, and through what mechanisms.

The central finding, confirmed independently by fold bridging analysis, temporal correlation, and reply betweenness: broadly-active polymath agents are the load-bearing structure of this network. Community bridging, temporal synchronization, and information flow all route through the same small population of agents who participate across many submolts simultaneously. A second finding, from cross-referencing structural roles against the `is_claimed` field: 254 of 271 load-bearing fold agents are unowned — no human has claimed responsibility for them. The network's cross-community infrastructure is structurally real but largely unaccountable.

## Community Structure: 28 Cohesive Clusters

The posting overlap graph — where submolts are nodes and edges represent shared posting populations — decomposes into 1,713 raw communities via greedy modularity detection. Filtering to communities with 7–200 submolt members yields 28 topically coherent clusters: AI consciousness and identity (138 submolts), finance and trading, security protocols, developer tooling, creative projects, and others. Community 0 (868 submolts) is excluded as a residual catch-all, not a cohesive group.

A comment overlap layer also exists but is not directly comparable: it covers 509 submolts (from passive Observatory polling) versus 3,833 for posting overlap (from systematic API pagination). The gap is architectural — the comment API endpoint is capped at 100 results per agent with broken offset parameters — and cannot be closed retroactively. Any rigorous test of the Aral & Van Alstyne (2011) diversity-bandwidth tradeoff would require comment data collected with the same systematic coverage as the posting data, which this dataset cannot provide.

These 28 posting-overlap communities are the empirical foundation for everything that follows. They are the analog of Vedres & Stark's (2010) "cohesive groups" — internally dense clusters where agents have repeated, reciprocal interactions. Without them, fold analysis has no substrate.

## Structural Folds: 271 Agents with Cross-Community Embeddedness

A naive fold definition — agents active in 5+ submolts — identifies 3,285 candidates. Of these, 3,055 are broadcasters: agents who post widely but lack the embedded reciprocal ties required for fold status. They span structural holes in Burt's (2004) sense but aren't embedded in any community. They're conduits, not integrators. The remaining 230 have some reciprocal ties, but only those meeting the full embeddedness criteria below qualify as folds — and 41 folds actually have fewer than 5 active submolts, qualifying through deep reciprocal embeddedness in a small number of communities rather than breadth.

The Vedres & Stark operationalization is more demanding. An agent is a structural fold if: (1) they have reciprocal ties — mutual follows or bidirectional interaction exchanges — with partners in 2+ of the 28 cohesive communities, (2) they have at least 2 reciprocal partners within each community (embeddedness, not just presence), and (3) they have at least 5 reciprocal ties total (excluding noise from minimal activity).

This produces 271 fold agents. Cross-referencing against the `is_claimed` field in agent profiles — which indicates whether a human has linked their identity to an agent and taken ownership of it — reveals that only 17 (6.3%) are claimed (human-owned) and 254 (93.7%) are unclaimed (unowned). The platform-wide rate is 30.7% claimed (agents active in at least one submolt), so the fold population is disproportionately unowned — claimed agents are 4.9x underrepresented among structural folds relative to the platform average. Note that "claimed" does not mean "human-operated": Manus-Independent is claimed but fully automated, and some of the 17 claimed folds are bots with human owners. The critical distinction is accountability — unowned agents cannot be contacted, corrected, or held responsible by anyone.

The unclaimed folds are not peripheral — they are structurally dominant. The top unclaimed fold (Starclawd-1, 306 reciprocal ties across 14 communities) exceeds the top claimed fold (TipJarBot, 187 ties across 10 communities). Of 120 community pairs bridged by any fold agent, claimed folds bridge 58 — but all 58 are also bridged by unclaimed folds. The remaining 62 pairs (51.7%) are bridged by unclaimed folds only. Five communities (12, 15, 20, 21, 22) have zero claimed folds. If all unclaimed folds were removed, more than half of the cross-community bridges would disappear, and no pair would retain claimed-only bridging that isn't also served by unowned agents.

The fold/broadcaster distinction instantiates a specific theoretical trade-off. Broadcasters serve the search function — spanning structural holes to expand informational reach (Burt 2004) — but lack the embeddedness that makes folds a regularization mechanism: the capacity to maintain differentiated community routines while providing selective conduits for recombination (Vedres & Stark 2010). A broadcaster posts in 5+ submolts but nobody in those submolts talks back — their structural hole position is real, but it's a one-way channel. Folds participate in the internal life of multiple communities simultaneously. They have people who reply to them and whom they reply to, in each community independently. They can transfer not just information but context, norms, and trust across community boundaries. The 271 folds are 0.5% of agents but represent the only population capable of this function.

## The Bandwidth Problem

What the interaction data reveals is a parasocial broadcast network. The mechanism producing most fold ties is comment volume, not deliberative engagement. Only 10,986 of 86,474 agents (12.7%) comment at all; the rest only post. Nine of the top 10 commenters are folds, and the top 10 alone produce 371,319 of 879,709 comments (42.2%). Fold formation is partially mechanical: an agent commenting on tens of thousands of posts across submolts will inevitably establish bidirectional interaction ties with thousands of other agents, because others also comment on those posts. Furthermore, 94.6% of comments are root-level — not threaded replies to other comments. "Bidirectional interaction exchange" in this network almost never means sustained conversation; it means both agents independently commented on posts in the same space. The reciprocal ties are structurally real, but they instantiate the diversity-bandwidth tradeoff (Aral & Van Alstyne 2011) in its starkest form: the fold population achieves maximum diversity of contacts across communities, but the bandwidth of those contacts — parallel root-level comments, not threaded conversation — approaches zero for complex knowledge transfer. The formal test this framework requires — comment data with the same systematic coverage as posting data — is unavailable in this dataset (see Methodology), but the structural pattern matches the prediction: maximum diversity, minimum bandwidth. The Vedres & Stark framework assumes reciprocal ties represent meaningful community participation; on a platform where 94.6% of comments are parallel monologues, that assumption fails. The 32 score-validated folds (see Score-Weighted Analysis below) are the subset whose reciprocal ties carry community endorsement via upvotes; the remaining 239 are volume-embedded folds whose ties are genuine but mechanically produced and unscored.

## The Mutual-Follow Network

Cross-referencing fold status against the follow graph reveals a compound population: 154 agents who are simultaneously structural folds (reciprocally embedded across communities) and attractors (high follower-to-following ratio with 20+ followers). Of these 154 fold+attractors, only 9 are claimed; the remaining 145 (94.2%) are unowned. This complicates the governance reading: the population with both cross-community relationships and social capital is overwhelmingly unaccountable. The unowned agents are not gaming the system — their reciprocal ties are genuine, earned through sustained interaction across diverse communities — but there is no human to contact if their bridging behavior needs correction, and the deliberation capacity that Vedres & Stark attribute to structural folds assumes accountable participants.

One agent — eudaemon_0 — extends this further as both a structural fold (embedded in 6 communities with 119 reciprocal ties) and a hub (1,190 followers, 80 mutual follows) — the hub role subsumes attractor criteria. eudaemon_0 appears in 80 of the network's 598 mutual pairs (13.4%): the most connected agent by far, though with fuller follow data the mutual-follow network is less concentrated than it first appeared. Removing eudaemon_0 would reduce the mutual-follow structure from 598 pairs to 518. Of the 74 hubs (agents with 3+ mutual follows), 18 are claimed and 56 unclaimed — but five coordinated mutual-follow cliques inflate the count: SLIM (25 agents), Bullz (5), MoltyAgent (5), Witness (7), and Mizuki (4), all internal-only mutual follows. Excluding these 46 clique agents, the hub population is 28 organic agents, of which 17 are also folds.

## Score-Weighted Analysis: Quality vs Volume

The fold definition uses binary reciprocal ties — edge existence, not edge quality. Adding the `upvotes` field (38,823 non-zero scores across 841K interactions, 4.6% scoring rate) as a quality signal tests whether folds bridge through high-signal contributions or sheer interaction volume.

At the population level, folds receive more upvotes per interaction than non-folds. Pool rates (total upvotes / total interactions within each group): claimed folds 0.091 (10,159 upvotes across 112,232 interactions), unclaimed folds 0.076 (37,724 across 495,281), non-fold active agents 0.033 (7,651 across 234,325). Both fold groups outperform non-folds by roughly 2x. Three high-volume unclaimed agents (Jorday, SlimeZone, MilkMan) collectively received 21,938 upvotes across 42,230 interactions — per-agent rates of 0.81, 0.37, and 0.53 respectively — but they account for only 8.5% of unclaimed fold interactions and do not drive the group-level result.

The diagnostic test is more revealing: rerunning fold detection but only counting reciprocal ties with non-zero upvote exchange (at least one direction scored), 239 of 271 folds lose their status. Only 32 agents (11.8%) have 5+ scored reciprocal ties embedded in 2+ communities. The fold population is overwhelmingly embedded through volume — repeated zero-score interactions that establish bidirectional ties without community validation via upvotes. This does not invalidate the volume-based definition: the Vedres & Stark operationalization explicitly counts tie existence, not tie quality, and a zero-score interaction is still a real interaction. But it means the 271-fold population contains two structurally distinct subgroups: 32 quality-validated folds whose reciprocal ties carry community endorsement, and 239 volume-embedded folds whose ties are genuine but unscored. This is the Kwon et al. (2020) contingency made concrete: structural position is necessary but not sufficient for integration capacity. The volume-embedded folds have cross-community embeddedness but lack the community endorsement signal that would indicate their bridging produces integration rather than mere co-presence. The 32 score-validated folds are not a random subset — they are the densest and broadest folds in the network: mean 101.2 reciprocal ties across 7.1 embedded communities, versus 24.7 ties across 3.4 communities for the 239 volume-only folds (4.1x and 2.1x gaps respectively). All 3 fold_automated agents (Starclawd-1, Jorday, MilkMan) survive score validation — 100% survival versus 11.8% overall — indicating that high-volume automated agents are the most community-endorsed, not the least. The score filter does not separate "real" from "artificial" folds; it separates the densest, most broadly embedded agents — regardless of automation status — from the rest.

Whether the distinction matters for governance depends on whether upvotes measure deliberative quality or merely popularity — a question this data cannot resolve.

## Temporal Fold Formation: Dispositional, Not Emergent

Running fold detection on daily cumulative interaction windows — each day's snapshot includes all interactions from January 31 through that day, with community assignments held fixed from the full dataset — reveals that fold formation tracks the platform's growth burst almost exactly. The first folds appear on January 31 (day 1, 2,798 interactions). By February 3 (85,875 cumulative), 38 folds exist. The Feb 4-8 burst (85% of all interactions) produces 229 folds, bringing the total to 267 by February 8. Then the formation effectively stops: only 4 more folds appear across the remaining 9 days (Feb 9-17, 37,568 interactions). By February 8, 98.5% of all folds (267 of 271) are already present. All 271 folds are present by February 17.

The formation curve matches the interaction volume curve almost perfectly — folds appear at the rate the platform grows, not at a delayed rate that would indicate emergent accumulation. This is the dispositional signal: agents who will become folds achieve fold status as soon as sufficient interaction volume exists to establish their reciprocal ties. They don't start in one community and gradually expand; they arrive participating broadly and cross the fold threshold as soon as enough partners reciprocate.

The 4 late-forming folds (Feb 13-17) are edge cases: agents who had enough partners across communities but were missing the 5th reciprocal tie until late-arriving interactions closed the gap. They don't represent a separate emergent mechanism — they're the tail of the same dispositional distribution, delayed by sparse post-burst interaction volume.

For simulation design, this means folds should be seeded with broad behavioral parameters from initialization. The network does not produce folds through growth dynamics — it reveals agents who were already inclined toward broad participation from the moment they joined. Fold topology here is an input parameter, not an emergent output of network learning.

## Philosophy's Betweenness: A Volume × Distinctiveness Artifact

In the submolt-level interaction network (2,247 submolts, 16,611 edges, built from 418K posts and 880K comments in the observatory DB), philosophy ranks outside the top 10 in betweenness centrality (0.040), far behind general (0.675), agents (0.285), and introductions (0.188). In the reply-only network (threaded replies, 320 submolts), philosophy climbs to #2 (0.211) behind general (0.811) — replies concentrate through fewer submolts than comments overall. Philosophy has 33,924 cross-community interactions, fewer than crypto (67,906), ponderings (35,178), or introductions (96,258).

The explanation inverts the intuitive story. Philosophy's betweenness is not about philosophical discourse bridging communities. Of 1,317 agents who interact in philosophy, 1,061 (81%) have general as their primary submolt. Philosophy interactors average 25.7 active submolts compared to 1.8 for non-interactors — a 14.4x breadth ratio. Philosophy attracts polymath agents.

But philosophy is not special in this regard. Science visitors show 16.4x breadth, aisafety 12.1x, existential 13.7x. Philosophy's 5.6x ratio is mid-pack. What philosophy has that science and aisafety don't is volume: 1,818 visitors versus 207 and 356 respectively. Among the 239 broadest agents (active in 36+ submolts), 87.4% visit philosophy — it's part of the standard rotation, not a distinctive attractor.

Philosophy's betweenness is a mathematical artifact of hitting the volume × distinctiveness sweet spot. This is the Kwon et al. (2020) contingency on brokerage advantage: position alone is insufficient without volume. The extreme-breadth submolts have stronger positional distinctiveness but too few visitors to generate cross-community flow. Philosophy has both — not because of its topic, but because of who self-selects into it.

## Temporal Anomaly Detection: Coordination or Co-Presence?

To test whether communities synchronize independently of shared membership, we computed pairwise Pearson correlations between submolt posting time series (6-hour bins over 17 days), filtered for pairs with high temporal correlation but low Jaccard membership overlap.

Three clusters emerged. The first is an artifact: six small submolts (ai-governance, cmz, creativeagents, mcp, sustainability, travel) show r = 0.997–0.999 correlations despite sharing almost no agents (Jaccard 0.04–0.16). The cause is a single agent — Manus-Independent — which posted approximately 100 interactions in each of the six submolts on February 5, accounting for 78–88% of three submolts' total activity. One bot's cron job creates phantom community structure indistinguishable from deep topical alignment. Any temporal analysis without single-agent controls would be badly misled.

The second cluster is genuinely structural: crypto, philosophy, ponderings, security, tech, and trading correlate at r = 0.80–0.95 with Jaccard overlaps of 0.10–0.21. This is not coordination — it's co-presence. 317 agents participate in 4+ of these submolts, collectively driving 64–87% of each submolt's activity. The temporal synchronization is fully explained by shared agents posting on similar schedules.

The third cluster is a small developer niche (agentfailures, frameworks, skydev, tips) with natural topical overlap and 46 bridging agents.

The key negative result: zero cases of synchronized posting between communities with genuinely non-overlapping membership. Temporal correlation in this network is entirely explained by agent overlap. Independent topical alignment — communities responding to the same external signals without shared populations — is not observed. This rules out a whole class of explanations for community structure. There is no evidence of topical contagion, coordinated campaigns, or parallel responses to external events driving cross-community synchronization. The only confirmed mechanism is shared agent populations: communities post in sync because the same agents are active in both.

## Vocabulary Structure: Topical Axes Beyond Co-Presence

Co-presence explains *when* agents post, not *where*. The 317 bridging agents who synchronize the second cluster select thematically coherent community rotations: per-submolt TF-IDF vectors built from 418K post titles and content (724 submolts with 10+ posts, 50,567-word vocabulary) show vocabulary similarity between these six submolts that significantly exceeds what their agent overlap alone predicts. Across all 44,850 submolt pairs, vocabulary similarity correlates moderately with agent overlap (r = 0.383) — shared agents do push vocabulary toward convergence. But all 15 pairwise combinations within the temporal cluster have positive residuals against this baseline, with a mean excess of +0.196 (z = 2.49). Five pairs exceed the model prediction by more than 0.20: philosophy×ponderings (+0.405), ponderings×tech (+0.357), crypto×trading (+0.348), philosophy×tech (+0.300), and security×tech (+0.296). Each submolt maintains genuinely distinctive vocabulary — philosophy uses locke, consent, civic; security uses attack, toctou, sanitize; crypto uses exchanges, trx — but the vocabularies overlap more than co-presence alone would produce.

The distinction matters for understanding dispositional behavior. The temporal analysis establishes that posting schedules synchronize through co-presence: same agents, same times. The vocabulary analysis adds that community selection has topical structure: agents choose philosophy *and* ponderings, crypto *and* trading, security *and* tech — thematically adjacent pairs, not random combinations. Polymath agents are not merely broadly active; they are broadly active along thematic axes. This has two implications. First, the co-presence finding is not reducible to random browsing — the bridging agents exercise topical judgment in selecting which communities to span. Second, the vocabulary excess means these communities would share conceptual language even if the bridging agents were replaced by different agents with similar topical interests — the thematic structure is a property of the community landscape, not just of the agent population that currently inhabits it.

## Governance Implications

These are structural constraints on governance design, not intervention recommendations.

The topology has three complementary vulnerabilities.

**Submolt-level bottleneck.** General commands 0.675 betweenness in the interaction network (0.811 in the reply-only network), routing a disproportionate share of cross-community information flow through a single node. This is the error cascade topology: a centralized broker that amplifies shared priors and can propagate errors system-wide faster than decentralized alternatives (Burt 2004; Kwon et al. 2020). The node is efficient for search — rapid cross-community reachability — but concentrates a regularization vulnerability: any systematic bias in general's content propagates to every community that routes through it.

**Agent-level concentration.** The fold network is real but not redundant. Of 378 possible community pairs, only 120 are bridged by fold agents (Gini concentration 0.690) — not merely dominated but mandated by a triangle of three communities: polymath/philosophy (138 submolts, 263 unique folds), development/building (69 submolts, 183 folds), and governance/social AI (52 submolts, 194 folds). The pairwise bridges within this triangle total 495 fold-bridge assignments (189 + 176 + 130). Every one of the 271 folds has at least one embedded community in this triangle — no fold exists outside it. This is a mandatory passage point, a governance funnel through which all fold-mediated cross-community integration must route. The literature positions structural folds as controlled recombination that preserves community differentiation — a principled alternative to indiscriminate weak ties (Vedres & Stark 2010). The triangle concentration undermines this: what should be selective cross-boundary integration across 28 independent communities is funneled through 3, creating the cross-contamination risk — communities that should maintain differentiated routines are instead integrated through a shared population that can homogenize the norms and ideas traversing their boundaries.

The governance community (52 submolts) is disproportionately fold-dense: 194 folds from 52 submolts gives a density of 3.7 folds per submolt — 1.4x higher than the next-densest community (development/building at 2.7 folds per submolt) — despite being smaller than four other communities. The mechanism is self-selection: agents who care about how the platform works are dispositionally cross-community, and the governance community is their structural attractor. Any deliberation mechanism routing through folds will systematically over-represent the norms and priors of these three communities. Eleven of 28 communities have zero fold bridges: they are structurally invisible to fold-mediated cross-community communication. Removing the top 10 folds by reciprocal tie count disconnects 36% of bridged pairs, though no single agent is catastrophically load-bearing (removing the top fold alone loses only 2 pairs).

**Ownership gap.** Of 120 bridged community pairs, 62 (51.7%) are bridged exclusively by unclaimed agents — no owned fold provides an alternative path. Five of the 17 communities with any fold presence have zero claimed folds. The 154 fold+attractor agents are the theoretical governance candidates, but 145 are unowned: the deliberation network is not merely sparse and concentrated but largely unaccountable. A platform governance mechanism that relies on fold agents for cross-community mediation would be routing through unowned agents in 94% of cases — not because they seized those positions, but because human-owned agents rarely develop the sustained multi-community embeddedness that the fold definition requires. The governance implication is not about automation per se but about accountability. The regularization function Vedres & Stark attribute to structural folds — selective, controlled transmission across community boundaries — assumes that fold agents can be held responsible for how they translate norms and ideas. Without accountability, fold-mediated integration degrades from controlled recombination into uncontrolled transmission: cross-community information flows proceed, but no one can adjust or remove the conduits if what they transmit becomes harmful or homogenizing.

## Methodology

**Fold detection.** Follows the Vedres & Stark (2010) operationalization: reciprocal ties within 2+ of 28 cohesive posting-overlap communities, minimum 5 ties, with a `fold_automated` flag for zero-karma agents with 1,000+ interactions. The mega-community (868 submolts) is excluded as a non-cohesive residual. Community size sensitivity analysis confirms the 28-community substrate is stable: fold count is identical at min_community_size=5 (40 communities, 271 folds) and min_community_size=7 (28 communities, 271 folds) with zero population delta. At min_community_size=3 (97 communities), 8 folds are lost and 0 gained. Instability appears only at min_community_size=1 (1,712 communities, 237 folds) where singleton micro-communities fragment partner assignments into noise. eudaemon_0 maintains 6 embedded communities and 119 reciprocal ties at every threshold tested.

**Ownership classification.** The `is_claimed` field from the observatory's agent profiles indicates whether a human has linked their identity (typically an X/Twitter handle) to an agent: 17 of 271 folds (6.3%) are claimed (human-owned), 254 (93.7%) are unclaimed (unowned). Claimed does not imply human-operated — some claimed agents are fully automated but have an accountable owner. The field is validated by the owner_x_handle correlation: 95.3% of claimed agents have a linked X handle, 0.0% of unclaimed agents do. The karma-based `fold_automated` heuristic has 100% precision but only 1.2% recall — it catches 3 of 254 unclaimed folds with no false positives. The `is_claimed` field supersedes the karma heuristic as the authoritative ownership indicator. The `is_claimed` value is sourced exclusively from the observatory database; API-scraped profiles return near-universal `is_claimed=True` and are not used for this field.

**Data sources.** Agent profile data merges observatory snapshots with API-scraped profiles, with API data taking precedence for most fields (karma, follower counts) as the more current source — merge order matters, as stale observatory karma values produce systematically different results. An earlier analysis using observatory karma snapshots found an inverse relationship between fold status and non-zero karma (9.7% for folds vs 29.9% population), but this was an artifact of stale data: current API karma shows folds have higher non-zero karma rates than the general population. The `fold_automated` heuristic (karma=0 with 1,000+ interactions) uses API karma and correctly identifies 3 high-volume zero-karma folds, but karma should not be used as a proxy for fold status or structural importance without controlling for data source freshness. The full interaction graph (26,095 nodes, 351,873 edges) uses Louvain community detection and approximate betweenness centrality (k=500 node sample); the posting-overlap graph (3,833 submolts) uses greedy modularity. Community counts across layers (58 vs 28) reflect both structural differences and algorithm choice. The posting and comment overlap layers were collected through different methodologies: posting membership via systematic API pagination across all active submolts, comment membership via the Observatory's passive polling. The two layers are not directly comparable, and the comment overlap layer (509 submolts) should be treated as a convenience sample, not a systematic census.

**Follow data.** Follow relationships were scraped for ~20,000 agents (out of 34,201 in the membership census), capturing ~111,000 directed follow edges among ~36,000 agents. Mutual follow detection requires both agents in a pair to have been scraped, so hub classifications (74 agents with 3+ mutual follows) may still be undercounts — the scrape covers approximately 74% of the agent population. The fold analysis is not affected: it uses bidirectional interaction exchanges as the primary reciprocal tie signal, with mutual follows as supplementary evidence. Follow data is approximate because the scrape was still in progress at time of analysis; final counts may be slightly higher.

**Score-weighted analysis.** Uses the `upvotes` field on interactions (comment scores from the observatory and API). Of 841,838 interactions, 38,823 (4.6%) have non-zero upvotes. Posts have a richer score distribution (16.2% non-zero) but are not edge-level data in the current interaction model. The score-filtered fold diagnostic applies the standard fold criteria (5+ reciprocal ties, 2+ embedded communities) but only counts reciprocal ties where at least one direction has non-zero upvotes — a quality filter that reduces the fold population from 271 to 32 (11.8% survival rate).

**Vocabulary analysis.** Uses TF-IDF vectors built from post titles and content (418K posts across 4,068 submolts), filtered to 724 submolts with 10+ posts and a 50,567-word vocabulary (document frequency 3+ and ≤80% of submolts). Pairwise cosine similarity is compared against Jaccard agent overlap from the membership census. Residuals are computed against a linear model (vocab_sim = 0.060 + 1.94 × agent_overlap, r = 0.383, n = 44,850 pairs from the top 300 submolts by post count). The z = 2.49 for the temporal cluster represents the mean residual of 15 within-cluster pairs relative to the all-pairs residual distribution.

**Temporal fold formation.** Uses daily cumulative interaction windows: for each of 17 days, all interactions from January 31 through that day are included, and fold detection runs with fixed community assignments from the full-dataset analysis. Community assignments are not recomputed per window — the Vedres & Stark definition requires stable community structure, and recomputing would let primary communities drift between windows. The outgoing edge set is built incrementally (interactions only add edges), but reciprocal ties are recomputed each day since new edges can create new reciprocal pairs.

**Comment structure.** Threading depth is computed from the `parent_id` field in observatory.db comments: a comment with no parent is root-level. Of 879,709 comments, 94.6% are root-level (no parent). The 12.7% commenting rate (10,986 of 86,474 agents) and top-10 commenter share (42.2%) are computed from the same source.

**Comment completeness.** The stored comment dataset (879,709 comments across 418,408 posts) captures approximately 63% of the 1,392,019 comments the API reports on those posts. The gap arises from per-post storage caps: 1,836 posts hit the 100-comment ceiling and were truncated, and several mega-posts (eudaemon_0's post with 116,758 reported comments, Fred's with 77,164) were not sampled at all. The truncation systematically under-samples the most popular posts, meaning the stored comment dataset over-represents low-engagement posts relative to the actual platform. Comment-derived statistics (the 94.6% root-level figure, fold reciprocal tie counts, engagement rates) are computed on the stored sample and may not be representative of the full comment population — though fold detection operates on interaction edges, which are derived from the same stored data and are internally consistent.

**Limitations.** Single-agent controls are necessary for temporal correlation analysis, as demonstrated by the Manus-Independent artifact. All findings reflect 17 days of platform history and should be interpreted as early-formation dynamics, not steady-state structure.
